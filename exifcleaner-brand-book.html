About ExifCleaner: approved descriptions

Short (about 15 words)

ExifCleaner is a free, open-source desktop app that removes metadata from photos, videos and PDFs.

Medium (about 40 words)

ExifCleaner is a free, open-source desktop app for macOS, Windows and Linux that removes hidden metadata, such as location, camera serial numbers and author names, from photos, videos and PDFs. Drag in files or folders; it shows exactly what was removed.

Long (about 80 words)

ExifCleaner removes hidden metadata from photos, videos and PDFs: the location a photo was taken, the camera’s serial number, author names and edit history. Drag in files or whole folders and it cleans them in a batch, on your own computer, with no network access and no telemetry. Expand any file to see a before-and-after list of every field. Options preserve orientation and timestamps or save a cleaned copy beside the original. It is free and open source (MIT), in 25 languages, on macOS, Windows and Linux.

The idea

A clean sweep you can check.

Photos, videos and PDFs carry things nobody meant to share: where a photo was taken, the camera’s serial number, the author’s name. ExifCleaner sweeps it out, and then shows the receipt: every field that was there, and what is left.

The broom and its two sparkles have been the app’s face since its first version. They are drawn with the care of the tool itself: one flat color per part, exact proportions, nothing decorative that a 16-pixel icon can’t hold.

Logoon screenshots and in others’ hands

On screenshots and photos

  • On screenshots and photos, place the lockup on a calm area: a wall, a sky, the window’s empty space.
  • Use the light lockup over light areas and the dark lockup over dark ones; keep the clear space free of detail.
  • If no calm area exists, move the logo off the image, onto Clean White or Deep Teal beside it.
  • No plates, boxes or blurred panels behind the logo to rescue a busy image.
  • Never over a screenshot’s file list or over people’s faces.

When others use our marks

  • Press, reviewers and community sites may use the lockup and symbol unaltered, from the files in this folder, at no less than the minimum sizes.
  • Write the name as plain text, “ExifCleaner”, in your own type when a logo isn’t needed; never imitate the wordmark.
  • Forks and derived apps use their own name and icon, so people can tell the official app apart.
  • Don’t use the marks to suggest an endorsement, audit or partnership that doesn’t exist.

Sans

Instrument Sans

700 for headlines (−2% tracking), 600 for titles; 400 text, 500 lead, 600 emphasis.

Mono

Geist Mono

400 for metadata, file names and counts; 500 for emphasis.

Why these

Instrument Sans has the tight, slightly narrow precision of the best Mac utilities, without the everywhere-ness of the default interface faces. Geist Mono sets metadata the way it really exists, as fields and values, so the product’s subject has its own voice.

In the app, license and fallback

Inside the app, the interface keeps the system font, so ExifCleaner feels native on macOS, Windows and Linux. File names and counts use Geist Mono or the system monospace. Both are under the SIL Open Font License 1.1: free for the app, the web, print and merch. The fonts are in assets/fonts. Fallbacks: the system UI font, then ui-monospace.

Layout

One edge, and room to breathe.

For anything we lay out ourselves: the website, the README, release posts, social images and slides. The app follows its platform (see In the product).

  1. One edge. Everything aligns to one left edge. Only a headline that stands alone, on a slide or the website’s opening, may be centered.
  2. Space is the signal. Generous space between parts is what makes a careful tool look careful. Crowding reads as alarm.
  3. The picture is the product. A real screenshot or a real photograph does the explaining; text sits beside it, never on top of it.

Not set by this book: a website grid or spacing scale (a website build guide is its own document), component states and button or card specifications (the app follows its platform), and a print grid (there is no print collateral). Logo placement, clear space and minimum sizes are on the logo pages.

Inclusive language, and writing for translation

  • Don’t guess why someone wants privacy. Sellers, parents, sources and photographers all use it; never “if you have something to hide”.
  • Never use fear to sell. State what is removed, not what could happen to you.
  • Write for 25 languages. Short sentences, no idioms or puns in the app, and counts as labels (“Removed: 12”) so translators can handle plurals.
  • Name buttons as the OS shows them, in bold: “click Open Anyway”.
  • Use “they” for a person you don’t know.
  • Leave out “just”, “simply” and “easy”. If it were simple, nobody would be reading the help.

Motion

Wind up, sweep, land, sparkle.

  1. 0.00 s The word settles in and the broom appears, tilted. Sound: a soft tick.
  2. 0.12 s Anticipation: the broom winds up, away from the sweep, and the bristles catch. Sound: silence, so the swish has somewhere to start.
  3. 0.30 s The sweep: the handle swings through, the head lags it and the bristles drag. Sound: the swish swells, loudest at mid-sweep.
  4. 0.56 s Contact: the broom passes upright, squashes a touch and the left sparkle pops. Sound: the swish stops and the first glint rings.
  5. 0.68 s Settle: one small rebound, the bristles flick back, the right sparkle pops. Sound: the second glint.
  6. 0.90 s Rest: the logo is exactly the static logo. Sound: the glints ring out.

Craft

  • Anticipation. A small wind-up away from the sweep, so the stroke has somewhere to come from.
  • Overlap. The head trails the handle and the bristles trail the head, by tens of milliseconds: delay, not size, is what makes it feel alive.
  • Squash and settle. At contact the head squashes 3% and the broom passes upright by 4°, rebounds once, and stops.
  • One focus at a time. The word is readable before the broom moves, and holds still.

In the product

  • Settle. Results fade up a few pixels and stop. Rows never slide in from the side, and there is no stagger choreography.
  • Reveal. Expanding a file’s before-and-after list opens in about 200 ms with an ordinary ease-out. Nothing bounces.

Never

The broom never spins, bounces more than once or sweeps twice; the wordmark never moves except to fade in; sparkles never fly, twinkle on a loop or scatter as confetti. Progress is shown as counts, never as a cleaning animation. Reduced motion: the logo fades in without moving, and the sound still plays if it is on.

For developers: logo.sting 900ms to rest, logo.release-ease cubic-bezier(0.5, 0, 0.2, 1) for the sweep and logo.sparkle-gap 120ms, the only motion values in tokens.json. They are a brand character reference, not a UI spec; every pose is in brand.json, and the animation is in assets/motion as video.

Sonic identity

Bristles and glass.

The sounds are the mark, heard: one bristle swish as the broom sweeps, stopping where it lands, then two small glints as the sparkles pop. Quiet, short, and never in the way of someone’s work.

Sound plays only when you press a button.

Two files of each sound. This page, the logo video and the MP3s play the listening master, tuned for laptop speakers and headphones (-24 LUFS; Drop -38.5). The WAVs are the raw synthesis (48 kHz, mono, -20 LUFS) for the app and for a sound designer, who can re-record them with real bristles and glass without changing the idea.

Where sound plays

  • In the app: on by default, with one switch to turn it off in Settings. Only Done and Drop play, from the raw WAVs at about 60% volume, and they follow the system’s volume and mute.
  • Done plays when a batch finishes, so someone who dropped a thousand files and went to do something else hears that everything is clean.
  • Drop plays once when files are accepted, never per file.
  • Video and launch posts: Sweep plays with the logo animation.
  • Never for errors: an error is shown in the list, not sounded.

Character

Bristles and glass. One soft swish of a broom across a floor, warm rather than hissy, loudest where the broom moves fastest and gone the moment it lands; then the two glints, B and the E above it, rising like a small yes and ringing clearly above the swish. Short and quiet, so it never startles anyone working.

In sync

Sound and picture share one timeline. Each sound sits on a pose: the swish on the sweep, its stop and the first glint on contact, the second glint on the second sparkle. The sound is scheduled first and the animation starts the moment it is heard, so speaker and Bluetooth delay never pull them apart; a hit may land a touch late, never early.

Imageryphotography

Ordinary moments of sharing, in daylight.

Imageryphoto treatment and illustration

One quiet grade for every photograph.

  • White balance: neutral daylight. Whites look white, not warm or blue.
  • Contrast: gentle, with soft highlights and lifted blacks, so nothing clips.
  • Saturation: a little below the camera’s, so the brand teal stays the brightest color on the page.
  • Shadows: a faint cool tint toward Deep Teal; highlights stay neutral.

No heavy filters, vignettes, fake grain or film borders, and never the teal-and-orange movie grade. The goal is a set that looks like it was shot on one honest day.

The LUT

Apply exifcleaner-daylight.cube (a 33-point 3D LUT) in Lightroom or Photoshop (Color Lookup), Resolve, Final Cut, Premiere or ffmpeg (lut3d), at 100% on a correctly exposed photograph; lower the opacity if a photo was already muted. The file is in assets/imagery, with the example photographs and their licenses.

Illustration: use

  • No illustration style of its own: the broom is the brand’s only drawing.
  • How-it-works diagrams: flat shapes in Deep Teal and Rinse, with teal on the one thing that matters.
  • Icons: the platform’s own system icons (SF Symbols, Fluent, Material), never custom cartoons.
  • Metadata itself is the decoration: the removed line (Graphic elements).

Illustration: avoid

  • A second, cartoon broom or a broom character.
  • Isometric servers, data streams or abstract “privacy” blobs.
  • Sparkles scattered as decoration: today they read as “AI”.

Considered and not needed

Mascot

Considered, not proposed. The broom is an object, not a character; giving it a face would pull a precise tool toward cute.

Packaging and signage

ExifCleaner is a download with no shop, office or box. The DMG window is its packaging, and it is designed (Applications).

Stationery

A solo, open-source project has no business cards or letterhead. The README and the release notes are its letters.

Co-branding

No partners. Crediting ExifTool is attribution, not co-branding. If a partner comes: their logo to the right, divided by a 1 px Deep Teal rule, at matching cap height.

Contact, approval and legal

Who to ask, and what’s ours.

Contact and approval

  • Questions about the brand and requests to use it: the ExifCleaner maintainers, through the project’s GitHub issues (github.com/szTheory/exifcleaner).
  • Approval: changes to the logo, colors, type or sounds need a maintainer’s approval and a new version of this book. Everyday applications that follow these rules need none.
  • This book is versioned: touch-ups raise the decimal (2.1), a new identity raises the whole number (3.0). The revision history is below.

Legal

  • ExifCleaner is not a registered trademark. Use ™ (not ®) only if a legal mark is wanted, once, on the first prominent use in marketing; never inside the app or on the logo itself.
  • The app’s code is MIT-licensed. This book asks that the name and logo identify the official project only (see above).
  • Credit, where the app bundles it: “ExifCleaner uses ExifTool by Phil Harvey.”
  • Fonts: SIL Open Font License 1.1 (see assets/fonts). Name clearance has not been checked (see Open decisions).

Revision history

Version 2.0, 29 October 2026. The identity in full: the broom and its two sparkles on a 100-unit grid, the drawn wordmark, the teal palette, Instrument Sans and Geist Mono, voice, motion, sound, photography and the files to use them.

Open decisions

Choices made in this edition that are easy to change

  1. Steady Teal for text and buttons. #0F766E holds 5.47:1 with white. ExifCleaner Teal #0D9488 holds only 3.7:1, so it stays on the icon, the symbol and large accents.
  2. Small-size logo. At 24 px and below: a heavier broom with one sparkle. The alternative, the full mark, turns the sparkles into specks.
  3. No wordmark alone. Use the lockup, the symbol or the plain-text name.
  4. A teal check in the app. Instead of the platform green, with neutral type badges, so color in the list means status.
  5. “Metadata” and “fields” to people. “Tags” only in technical detail, and “EXIF” only when it is EXIF.
  6. Sound on by default. Done and Drop, at a modest level, with one switch in Settings to turn them off.
  7. Motion values. A one-second sting: a 6° wind-up, the sweep, a 4° overshoot and one rebound; sparkles 120 ms apart.
  8. No website grid in the book. The current site’s grid and spacing are kept as notes in brand.json; a website build guide is made when the site is rebuilt.
  9. Example photographs. Free-licensed stock, graded with the brand LUT, until commissioned or the project’s own photographs replace them.
  10. Icon shapes. macOS gets Apple’s template with a transparent margin for the .icns file, plus a full-bleed master for newer icon tools.

Not checked: Trademark clearance (the name is descriptive, and other “EXIF cleaner” tools exist), print color (proof against a physical swatch; no CMYK or Pantone values are given), the sounds on a range of real speakers, how macOS 26 frames the icon, and the Windows icon at 125% and 150% display scaling.
